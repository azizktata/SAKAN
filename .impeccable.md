# SAKAN Design Context

## Design Context

### Users

Three overlapping personas — design must serve all simultaneously without diluting the signal for any:

1. **Tunisian family buyer** — Middle-class family that has saved for years. Lands on the homepage with hope and fear mixed together. Needs immediate legitimacy signals: "this is real, this is for us, this is trustworthy." Will exit at the first sign of complexity or shadiness.
2. **Young Tunis professional** — 25–35, tech-comfortable, urban. Wants speed and directness. Respects bold design. Has low tolerance for friction or "marketing speak."
3. **Diaspora buyer** — Tunisian abroad (France, Gulf) investing remotely. Most skeptical. Needs strong credibility signals, clear pricing, and a sense that the platform understands Tunisia specifically.

All three share one need: **trust at first sight.** The design must feel like a serious, credible institution that belongs to Tunisia — not a foreign platform copied in Arabic.

### Brand Personality

**Trustworthy · Premium · Tunisian**

Three adjectives, each load-bearing:
- **Trustworthy**: not minimalist-cold; warmth earns trust. Credibility through density and substance, not through whitespace.
- **Premium**: not luxury-foreign. Premium means "this was made with care and intention for you." Quality materials, not marble lobbies.
- **Tunisian**: culturally specific. Architecture, light, stone, terracotta, olive groves — not Islamic decoration, not generic MENA vibes. The specificity of a place.

Emotional goal: a Tunisian family should feel the same mix of **pride and confidence** they'd feel walking into the best local bank. "This is ours, and it's good."

### Aesthetic Direction

**Bold, layered, editorial — not minimal, not empty.**

The current design is too quiet. The redesign should have visual weight, typographic authority, and compositional confidence. Every section should feel *intended*, not just present.

**References feel**: Architectural Digest Middle East (editorial weight) × a serious North African newspaper masthead (typographic authority) × a well-built European property portal (functional density).

**Anti-references — never look like:**
- Generic SaaS / startup: no Inter everywhere, no blue hero gradients, no equal-padding card grids
- Islamic visual clichés: no arabesque borders, no calligraphy as decoration, no crescent icons
- Western luxury real estate: no white marble, no Sotheby's serif-heavy "old money" look
- Dark mode crypto: no neon glows, no glassmorphism as default, no purple/cyan on dark
- **Empty minimalism**: blank white space is not a design decision. Every section must earn its presence with visual substance.

**Theme**: Light mode (Tunisian real estate is browsed in daylight, on mobile, by people who trust light UIs).

### Design Principles

1. **Substance over space** — dense information is not the enemy; poorly organised information is. Pack sections with real content; let hierarchy do the separation.
2. **Tunisian specificity** — the palette, imagery choices, and typography must be rootable in North Africa. No visual could be mistaken for Dubai, Paris, or Silicon Valley.
3. **Credibility through confidence** — large, assured type. Solid color blocks. No apologetic sizing or tentative micro-text for primary messages.
4. **Bold but never loud** — boldness is compositional authority, not shouting. Controlled asymmetry, strong typographic scale, deliberate accent use.
5. **Every section earns its place** — each landing page section must have a distinct visual identity AND a clear job. No decorative filler sections.

### Font Strategy

**Brand words**: warm · authoritative · rooted

Reject all reflex_fonts (Inter, Fraunces, DM Sans, etc.). Suggested direction:
- **Display**: A typeface with editorial authority and slight geometric personality — something that reads as "serious publication" not "startup product." Look at: Reforma (Argentine humanist serif with modernist forms), Canela (editorial slab-influenced), Sentient, Editorialist, Roslindale, or similar. Must be available on Google Fonts or self-hostable.
- **Body**: A humanist sans with warm numerals and good Arabic-adjacent rhythm — look at Geist, Nunito Sans (if not overused in context), Lexend, or a text-optimized grotesque.

### Color Strategy

**Reject the current muted forest green.** The new palette must be more assertive.

Direction: **Terracotta-anchored warm palette with deep olive as authority color.**

- Primary authority color: deep olive-green (`oklch(32% 0.08 130)` range) — serious, rooted, North African
- Accent / warmth: burnt terracotta (`oklch(58% 0.14 45)` range) — the color of Tunisian stone walls in afternoon light
- Surface: warm off-white with terracotta tint (`oklch(97% 0.01 70)`) — never pure white
- Text: near-black with warm undertone (`oklch(14% 0.02 70)`)
- Contrast accent: deep amber / saharan gold for premium signals

This palette is **visually specific to North Africa** — not generic "earthy" but actually matching the tonal vocabulary of Tunis medina stone, Sidi Bou Saïd blue-and-white, and Saharan ochre.
